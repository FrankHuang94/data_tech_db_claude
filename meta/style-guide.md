# Style guide

## Reader and voice

- Reader: a corporate-development / strategy executive (CFA, MBA, law, semiconductor and AI-infra M&A) joining Everpure (formerly Pure Storage). Smart, numerate, time-poor, not a database engineer.
- Voice: a sharp senior analyst briefing an executive. Plain English, short paragraphs (2–5 sentences), concrete worked examples, real numbers, balanced judgments. State a view, then the strongest counter-argument.
- Avoid hype words ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "unlock"). Avoid vendor marketing phrasing; describe what the thing actually does.
- Define every acronym on first use in each chapter, then link it to the glossary the first time (`[LSM-tree](../glossary.md#lsm-tree)`).
- Depth: L2 by default. L3 only inside `> **Going deeper (L3).**` callouts.
- Analogies: use the reader's home turf — semiconductors and memory hierarchies (SRAM/DRAM/NAND, caches, prefetching, wafers/yield), datacenter infrastructure, storage (companion repo), and finance/valuation (royalties, annuities, options, NRR as cohort analysis, cap tables). Mark them `> **Bridge: …**`. One or two strong analogies beat five weak ones.

## Numbers and labels

- Every number: inline footnote with source, published date, retrieved date.
- Label every financial figure: **GAAP**, **non-GAAP**, **company-stated run-rate**, **press-reported**, or **estimate**. Write it in the sentence or table cell, e.g., "$3.6B product revenue (GAAP, FY2025 = Feb 2024–Jan 2025)".
- Fiscal years: always explicit. Snowflake FY ends Jan 31 (FY2026 = Feb 2025–Jan 2026). MongoDB FY ends Jan 31 (FY2026 = Feb 2025–Jan 2026). Everpure/Pure Storage FY ends on the Sunday closest to Feb 1 (approx.; FY2026 ≈ Feb 2025–Jan 2026) [check filings]. Oracle FY ends May 31. Microsoft FY ends Jun 30. Salesforce FY ends Jan 31. Calendar-year companies: say "CY2025" or "FY2025 (calendar)".
- Distinguish: **ARR** vs **revenue run-rate** (annualized quarterly or monthly revenue) vs **product revenue** vs **total revenue**; **valuation** (post-money, private) vs **market cap** vs **enterprise value**.
- Valuation multiples: give date and basis ("EV/NTM revenue ≈ 14x, as of 2026-10-31, NTM from company guidance").
- Currency: US$ unless stated. Use "$1.2B", "$350M", "$62B". Percentages: "27%". Dates: ISO in footnotes (2026-03-05); "March 2026" in prose.
- Single source of truth: numbers that appear in `data/*.csv` must match the CSV. If you find a better or newer figure, propose it in `data/staging/` and use it consistently.
- Unverifiable: `[VERIFY: what needs checking]` inline, and add a line in `meta/open-questions.md`.

## Copyright

- Write in your own words. No pasted vendor or press text. Quotes < 15 words, max one per source, always attributed.
- Never reproduce vendor diagrams; redraw concepts as original Mermaid/SVG.

## Visuals

- At least 1 visual per ~700 prose words and ≥3 per chapter, of which ≥2 should be diagrams or charts (not only tables).
- **Mermaid first.** Allowed types: `flowchart`, `sequenceDiagram`, `timeline`, `mindmap`, `quadrantChart`, `xychart-beta`, `stateDiagram-v2`, `classDiagram`, `erDiagram`, `gantt`, `pie`. Do **not** use `block-beta`, `architecture-beta`, `packet-beta`, `sankey-beta` or `kanban` (inconsistent support on github.com).
- Mermaid hygiene (prevents render failures on GitHub and MkDocs):
  - Quote any node label containing punctuation: `A["Parquet files (S3)"]`. Use `<br/>` for line breaks inside quoted labels.
  - No `click` directives, no `%%{init}%%` theme overrides, no emojis, no `&` or `#` in unquoted labels; avoid `;` inside labels.
  - Keep diagrams ≤ ~25 nodes; use `subgraph` for layers. Prefer `flowchart LR` for pipelines and `flowchart TB` for stacks.
  - `timeline` and `mindmap`: keep text plain (no parentheses/colons inside items except the timeline `period : event` separator).
  - `xychart-beta`: use `x-axis [..]` with quoted category labels and a single `bar` or `line` series per call.
  - Validate with `scripts/lint_mermaid.sh <file>`.
- **Charts**: generated by `scripts/make_charts.py` from `data/*.csv` into `docs/assets/charts/<name>.png`. Never hand-edit a chart PNG.
- **SVG** for dense layered diagrams (market map, stack maps) in `docs/assets/diagrams/`. Hand-written SVG must be original, use the palette below, and include `<title>` and `<desc>`.
- **Captions and alt text**: every visual is followed by a caption line in italics that states the takeaway, numbered per chapter:
  `*Figure 7.2 — Iceberg commits are a pointer swap in the catalog, which is why the catalog becomes the control point.*`
  Images use descriptive alt text: `![Bar chart of Snowflake product revenue FY2020–FY2026 showing growth slowing from 100%+ to ~30%](../assets/charts/snowflake_revenue.png)`.
- Tables count as visuals only when they are structured comparisons (≥3 rows).

## Links

- Relative links only. From a chapter to another part: `../04-company-deep-dives/ch25-databricks.md`. To the glossary: `../glossary.md#term-slug` (slug = lowercase, spaces→hyphens, punctuation removed).
- Companion repo: refer to it as "the companion *Everpure Tech Atlas*" (no URL is known; do not invent one).

## Formatting conventions

- Chapter header line exactly as in `meta/templates.md`.
- Reading time ≈ prose words / 230, rounded.
- "Last verified: 2026-11-01" unless you verified on another date.
- Use `<details><summary>…</summary>…</details>` for flashcards and optional asides.
