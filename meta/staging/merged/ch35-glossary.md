- **Consumption pricing** — Charging per unit of work done (credits, DBUs, bytes scanned, rows), with revenue recognised as the customer consumes. (ch35)
- **Seat-based pricing** — Charging per named user or developer for a subscription term, recognised ratably. (ch35)
- **Open core** — A business model that gives away an open-source engine and sells enterprise features, support or a hosted service around it. (ch35)
- **Capacity commitment** — A customer's multi-year promise to buy a dollar amount of usage at set prices, invoiced in advance and drawn down as consumed. (ch35)
- **Capacity-based pricing** — Charging for reserved throughput or compute capacity rather than for volume processed. (ch35)
- **Rollover** — A contract term that carries unused prepaid capacity into a later order, usually when the customer buys more. (ch35)
- **Overage** — Usage above a commitment, billed at an on-demand or pre-agreed rate. (ch35)
- **Breakage** — Revenue a vendor recognises when prepaid capacity expires unused. (ch35)
- **Ratable recognition** — Spreading subscription revenue evenly over the contract term regardless of usage. (ch35)
- **Remaining performance obligations** — Contracted revenue a company has not yet recognised (RPO), including unconsumed capacity commitments. (ch35)
- **Product revenue** — Snowflake's consumption-recognised revenue line, which excludes professional services. (ch35)
- **Private offer** — A negotiated price sold through a cloud marketplace, often counting toward the buyer's cloud spending commitment. (ch35)
- **Optimization cycle** — A period when customers systematically cut usage-based bills, compressing consumption vendors' growth, as in 2022–23. (ch35)
- **Efficiency headwind** — Revenue a consumption vendor loses when its own performance improvements reduce the units customers burn. (ch35)
- **Jevons paradox** — The observation that efficiency gains which lower unit cost can increase total consumption of a resource. (ch35)
- **Snowflake credit** — Snowflake's unit of compute consumption, burned per hour by warehouse size and priced per edition. (ch35)
- **DBU** — Databricks Unit, a normalised unit of processing capacity per hour, priced per workload SKU. (ch35)
- **Slot** — BigQuery's unit of query compute capacity, billed per slot-hour under editions. (ch35)
- **RPU** — Redshift Processing Unit, the per-hour compute billing unit of Amazon Redshift Serverless. (ch35)
- **eCKU** — Confluent Cloud's elastic capacity unit, billed per hour for Kafka cluster capacity. (ch35)
- **Monthly active rows** — Fivetran's meter of distinct rows inserted, updated or deleted per month, tiered per connection. (ch35)
- **Informatica Processing Unit** — Informatica's pre-paid, pooled consumption unit spendable across IDMC services. (ch35)
- **Flex Credits** — Salesforce's consumption currency for Agentforce, priced per agent action. (ch35)
- **Serverless compute** — Vendor-operated compute billed per use, with the cloud infrastructure cost inside the vendor's cost of revenue. (ch35)
- **Bring your own cloud** — A deployment model (BYOC) in which vendor software runs in the customer's cloud account, so the customer pays the infrastructure directly. (ch35)
- **Take-or-pay** — A contract that obliges the buyer to pay for a minimum volume whether or not it is used, as in foundry wafer agreements. (ch35)
- **Subscription ARR** — Everpure's KPI: annualised value of active non-cancellable subscriptions plus the latest quarter's on-demand billings times four. (ch35)
- **Evergreen One** — Everpure's storage-as-a-service offering (Evergreen//One), billed on a reserve commitment plus on-demand usage with service-level guarantees. (ch35)
